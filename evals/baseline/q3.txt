Initech's shift from perpetual licenses to subscription is going well overall, though there's a recent accounting issue worth watching.

**The mix shift is on track.** Subscription revenue hit 62% of total revenue in FY2024, and management's preliminary FY2025 guidance (from the Jan 30, 2026 8-K) points to roughly 68%. Meanwhile, perpetual license revenue has shrunk to 19% of the total, declining 19% year-over-year in FY2024 — right on plan. Notably, management says it hasn't discounted to rush customers into converting, which is the right call for a durable transition even if it means slower near-term revenue.

**Growth and customer retention look healthy.** Total revenue has grown at a steady low-to-mid-teens pace for years: $640M (FY21) → $742M → $861M → $988M (FY24), each about 14–16% higher than the prior year. In FY2024 specifically, subscription revenue grew 26% while perpetual declined 19% — exactly the pattern you'd want to see during this kind of transition. Net revenue retention of 112% shows existing subscription customers are spending more over time, a good sign for the durability of the recurring revenue base. Gross margin has also been climbing each year (73.6% to 74.8% from FY21-24), which tracks with subscription's richer margins, and both operating income and free cash flow have scaled right along with revenue.

**FY2025 guidance** calls for revenue of $1.12–1.14B (13-15% growth) and subscription mix rising further to about 68% — consistent with the multi-year trend.

**One caveat:** these FY2025 numbers are preliminary and unaudited. Initech has said its 10-K will be delayed because it's reviewing revenue recognition on a batch of multi-year contracts signed in Q4 2025. That's worth watching closely, since it bears directly on subscription revenue recognition — the central metric in this whole transition story. The company itself warns final results "may differ materially" from the preliminary range. Separately, there's a past restatement to keep in mind: FY2022 segment revenue was reallocated due to a reporting-structure change (it didn't affect consolidated totals), so be cautious comparing segment-level figures across years.

**Bottom line:** the transition looks strong across growth, mix, margin, and retention — but the unresolved revenue-recognition review on those Q4 2025 contracts is a real near-term risk that could affect how much confidence to place in the preliminary subscription numbers until the delayed 10-K comes out.
